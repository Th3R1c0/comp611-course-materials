[DS-1] Disjoint sets and the three operations

Groups that share no items. Each group has a representative. MAKE-SET(x) new group {x}. FIND-SET(x) gives x's representative. UNION(x, y) joins the two groups. Same representative means same group.Example: {1, 2, 5}, {3, 4}, {6}. FIND-SET(5) and FIND-SET(1) match, so 1 and 5 are in the same group.

Uses: connected components (UNION both ends of every edge) and Kruskal's cycle check (same root for both ends means the edge would make a cycle).

[DS-2] Forest and FIND-SET

Each group is a tree. Every node points to its parent; the root points to itself and is the representative. FIND-SET: follow arrows up to the root.Example: 1 to 2 to 4 to 6, and 6 is the root, so FIND-SET(1) = 6. The find path is 1, 2, 4, 6.

The nodes you pass on the way up are called the find path.

[DS-3] Path compression

After FIND-SET(x), every node on the find path points straight at the root. Nodes not on the path do not change. Ranks do not change.Example: FIND-SET(1) on path 1, 2, 4, 6: 1 and 2 now point to 6. 4 already did.

This makes later finds on those nodes take one step.

[DS-4] Union by rank

Find both roots first. Different ranks: smaller rank goes under bigger, no rank change. Tie: the first root goes under the second root, and the second root's rank goes up 1. New items have rank 0.Example: UNION(a, b) with both rank 0: a under b, b rank 1. Then root 4 (rank 1) and root 9 (rank 3): 4 goes under 9, rank 9 stays 3.

Rank is an upper limit on the tree's height. Only a tie grows it.

[DS-5] Running times

Versionm operations
Forest, no tricksO(m n)
Union by rank onlyO(m log n)
Rank + path compressionO(m α(n))
Linked lists, weighted unionO(m + n log n)
Example: α(n) is the inverse Ackermann function, at most 4 for any real n, so almost O(m).

Linked lists: MAKE-SET and FIND-SET are O(1), UNION costs the length of the list that is moved.
